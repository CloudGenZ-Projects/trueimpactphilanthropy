Fundraising Strategy

How to build a fundraising strategy when your nonprofit has limited time and capacity.

Small nonprofits often try to do too much: grants, events, corporate outreach, major gifts, social media campaigns and donor appeals — all at once. A useful strategy begins by choosing where not to spend limited capacity.

Start with your current revenue. Which sources are stable? Which are declining? Which depend on one person or one funder? Then examine your strongest relationships: donors, volunteers, board members, corporate contacts, foundations, community partners and program participants.

What should nonprofit leaders consider?

Choose two or three priority revenue areas for the next 90 days. Give each one an owner, a target, specific next actions and a measure of progress. Build stewardship into the plan rather than treating it as something that happens after fundraising.

What this means in practice

A strategy should make decisions easier. If every new opportunity automatically becomes a priority, you do not yet have a strategy — you have a list.
